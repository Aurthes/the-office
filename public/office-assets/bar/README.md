# Listening bar surfaces

The bar and turntable use app-authored Three.js geometry. Textures are pinned locally at 1K resolution; no runtime CDN requests are needed.

- `wood-*.jpg`: [Wood Table 001, Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/a/wood_table_001), [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license). Original color, OpenGL normal and roughness maps from `https://dl.polyhaven.org/file/ph-assets/Textures/jpg/1k/wood_table_001/wood_table_001_{diff,nor_gl,rough}_1k.jpg`.
- `marble-*.jpg`: [Marble 006, ambientCG](https://ambientcg.com/view?id=Marble006), [CC0 1.0](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/). Original color, OpenGL normal and roughness maps from `https://ambientcg.com/get?file=Marble006_1K-JPG.zip`.

The bar is in the main deck's observation lounge, with the central crew lane left clear. `/bar-preview` starts focused on that room. The Music label appears on mouse hover or keyboard focus; touch users tap the turntable directly.

Choose NetEase Cloud Music to search and play through the official API inside Office. Developer credentials and account authorization stay on the server; see [credential setup](../../../docs/netease-credentials.md). Sign-in uses a locally generated QR or an explicit official authorization link for phone users. Closing the panel, pressing Escape, or clicking its backdrop keeps the same audio playing as background music. A compact control panel floats over the camera view with the song, pause/resume, volume and a separate Stop button. Click the song or turntable to reopen the full player. Returning to the chooser stops playback; Reload restarts the player. Account entitlements and preview limits still apply.

The earlier full-site iframe prototype could not complete sign-in because NetEase's login and discovery pages forbid cross-origin framing. The API player replaces that prototype.

The NetEase player opens with scene recommendations for the current star system: space ambient, glacial atmospheres, desert atmospheres, ocean instrumentals, or science-fiction game scores. These recommendations come from matching NetEase playlists, with pop, metal, rap and dance playlists excluded when identified in playlist metadata. If a scene has no playable songs, a broader space ambient mix is used. The separate Daily recommendations tab uses the account's actual NetEase daily list. Scene mixes and daily lists omit tracks marked unavailable by the provider; search keeps them visible with an explanation.

Scene playlists must explicitly identify instrumental music, pure music or no vocals. Before a scene track receives an audio URL, the server checks `/openapi/music/basic/song/lyric/get/v2`: `pureMusic === true` or `noLyric === true` must confirm it is instrumental. Vocal and unconfirmed tracks are skipped, including when advancing automatically. Empty lyric text, a missing language or `vocalFlag` are not accepted as proof. This verification applies to scene background music; explicit daily-list and search playback keep their normal behavior.

Play a mix or select a song to start a queue. At the end of a track, playback advances automatically, skips tracks that have become unavailable, and fetches another collection when the queue runs out. A new star system changes the next scene collection without interrupting the current song. Browsing another list does not replace the playing queue until a song or Play mix is selected. The compact camera control is one row, with next-track and a volume popover. Only Pause or Stop ends continuous listening; network errors or an exhausted playable catalog may require retrying.

Entering Office automatically loads and starts the current scene mix when NetEase is already authorized. The full player stays closed; the compact controls appear over the camera. If the browser blocks audible autoplay, the controls show a play hint and the next click or keypress outside the music controls retries playback in that user gesture. The music controls themselves retain their explicit play/pause/stop behavior. Pause and Stop are respected for the current visit, including recommendation refreshes and scene changes; reloading Office starts a new visit. This follows the [browser autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay/).
